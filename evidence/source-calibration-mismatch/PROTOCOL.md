# Distributed-source reference-calibration audit, frozen development design

## Question and identifiability

The current one-plane route fits `F(s,v;r)=(|r-H_n(s)|-|r-s|)/v` to a single eligible recorded echo per receiver, with independently held receivers. For two simultaneous emitters s and s+delta, each direct and reflected component has its own range. A mixed direct/reflected pair generally cannot be represented by one source and the known plane. When delta is parallel to the reference plane and receiver bearings make projected range differences small, the two components can overlap in both windows. A good effective point fit can then be specific to that geometry/band, rather than establish a universally valid physical center.

For exact receivers on the perpendicular bisector of a tangential emitter separation, both direct distances coincide and both image distances coincide. That receiver set is planar; the existing noncoplanarity guard rejects this exact case. Nearby noncoplanar receivers remove the exact equality but may leave inadequate practical discrimination. A source pair with the same frequency response and sufficiently small projected delays can behave approximately like an amplitude-weighted center. At24cm separation over2–14kHz, this approximation is not uniformly justified; the raw experiment must determine whether it actually passes or fails. No source calibration success is assumed.

A second independently surveyed reference plane with normal having a component along delta changes the image-source separation direction and can separate a hidden tangential pair. It is a justified discriminating experiment, not a universal guarantee: a separation parallel to both planes' intersection can remain weakly constrained, and source/receiver bandwidth, bearing diversity and phase determine observability. Three independent directions or qualified source routing may be necessary in general. Known reference planes remain supplied calibration geometry, never recovered room structure.
